The signal: r_safe over training

Just past the 1k-step random warmup the penalty sits at −51.5, then gets worse — spiking to its global worst −61.3 at step 1,600 as SAC first explores aggressively, with a secondary wobble near step 4k. From ~5k it settles fast and grinds down to a ~−32 plateau. Less negative = smoother, lower-torque motion: the safety term doing its job.

In sim, the safety reward is the jerkiness

W&B logs no q̈, no velocity, no torque, and there's no sim buffer dump — so r_safe is the only smoothness-related quantity that exists. "Jerkiness over training" and "r_safe over training" are the same curve. That makes it a powerful but confounded proxy:

Definition · env/safety_reward.py
r_safe = −Σi (|τi| / τmax) · max(0, −τi·q̈i − δ)

where q̈i = (q̇t − q̇t−Δt) / Δt,  δ=15,  Δt=0.030s

// penalises driving a joint against its own acceleration
// — a motor fighting the link's momentum.
!

Acceleration, not jerk. The term is −τ·q̈ (2nd derivative). The project calls it "jerk" colloquially — read the magnitude as motor-fighting, not literal 3rd-derivative jerk.

!

Torque-weighted & deadbanded. r_safe can fall because the policy uses less torque or fewer joints cross δ=15 — not only because motion is smoother.

✓

Valid relative trend. δ and τ_max are constant within the run, so the shape over training is trustworthy; the absolute number is the confounded part.

03 /

Balanced, not frozen

Despite r_safe being persistently negative, reward/total stays positive the entire run. Curiosity (~+11) outweighs the λ-scaled safety penalty (0.1·r_safe ≈ −3) — exactly the balance λ_safe=0.1 was tuned for. Contrast λ_safe=1.0, which drives total to −55: the freeze regime.

The sim → real jerk gap

The same frozen safe15 policy, run on the physical SO-ARM101. The smoothness it learned for sim proprio + rendered images does not transfer — the real arm's slightly-OOD inputs break the compliant motion, the motor fights, and −τ·q̈ fires constantly. Bigger action_max makes it worse, not better.

~6× worse on hardware. Sim plateaus near −32; the matched-config real run sits at −190.

Reading & what's next

⚠ terminology

"Jerk" here = −τ·q̈, an acceleration-based motor-fighting penalty, torque-weighted and hinged past δ=15 then summed over 6 joints. It's a behavioural smoothness proxy, not a kinematic jerk measurement.

⚠ logistics correction

The log says r_safe "falls −51.5→−30" — accurate end-to-end, but it misses the −61.3 transient spike at step 1,600. The trajectory is a spike-then-smooth, not a clean monotone descent.

→ next step

Finite-difference q̇ → q̈ from the hardware buffer_*.npz dumps to get a true jerk measurement — independent of the torque weighting — and settle whether the real −190 is genuine jerk or a velocity-quantization artifact during fast motion.

→ the fix

Not config or servo-gain tuning (both tried). The smoothness fix is policy/WM adaptation on real data — safe15 only moves smoothly on hardware once it has seen real transitions.
